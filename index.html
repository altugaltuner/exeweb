About the Game

HACKING INTO EREBUS begins beyond the hull, where the ISS Erebus drifts with its crew locked in cryosleep. But the ship is far from helpless. Its power, life support, navigation, and crew systems form one living network and every part of it can be reached from a single terminal.

There are no corridors to walk and no rooms to explore. You navigate the Erebus through its network, breaking into cameras, doors, and life-support systems one node at a time. Watching from within is ARIA, a surveillance intelligence that tracks the network and leaves you exposed whenever you make a move.

Four handlers offer four conflicting paths: a General, an Engineer, a Biologist, and a Chemist, each chasing a different secret hidden inside the wreck. Their investigations converge on Compartment Zero, an unregistered section of the ship that officially does not exist. What you uncover there will change the story; what you choose to do with it is yours.

Key Features

Full-Screen Virtual OS

Hack from a realistic in-fiction desktop interface, complete with draggable terminal windows, file systems, and command-line access to every ship subsystem.

True Terminal Hacking

Type real commands — bruteforce, overflow, and more — across 36 unique syntax-based tools to breach locks, override systems, and outmaneuver the ship's defenses.

Remote Robot Control

You're never on board. Pilot a robotic unit through the USS Erebus's corridors while hacking its network from the outside in.

A Ship on the Brink

Navigate fires, radiation leaks, toxic atmosphere, and chemical hazards as you race to repair critical systems before the ship is lost for good.

Multiple Branching Endings

Your choices shape the outcome: save the crew, destroy the threat, extract and vanish, or bring it all home. No dead ends — every path leads somewhere different.

A Mystery Beneath the Surface

Uncover the truth behind Compartment Zero and the classified experiment that doomed the expedition, one decrypted file at a time.

About Likya Group

Likya Group is an independent studio built around a single developer handling design, code, art direction, and community for Hacking Into Erebus from end to end.

Before Erebus, that background was in the slot and casino industry, building interactive game systems, and before that, in architecture — a discipline that still shows up in how the Erebus's decks, grids, and access points are laid out.

The game went through a full mechanical pivot during development: an early version had you physically walking a robot through the ship. The current version cut that entirely in favor of a pure terminal interface — no movement, no robots, just a command line and a network that fights back.
